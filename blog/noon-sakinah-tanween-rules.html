Noon Sakinah & Tanween Rules in Tajweed

Noon Sakinah and Tanween are important parts of Tajweed because the sound of the letter Noon can change depending on the letter that follows it. Learning these rules helps Quran students recite more accurately and recognize when a Noon Sakinah or Tanween should be pronounced clearly, merged, changed or partially concealed.

There are four main rules for Noon Sakinah and Tanween: Izhar, Idgham, Iqlab and Ikhfa. Understanding these four rules gives students a practical foundation for improving Quran pronunciation and applying Tajweed while reading.

What Is Noon Sakinah?

Noon Sakinah is the letter Noon (ن) with a sukoon. It has no vowel sound and can occur in the middle or at the end of a word. When another Arabic letter comes after Noon Sakinah, the pronunciation follows one of the four Tajweed rules.

Recognizing Noon Sakinah is an important first step because the following letter determines which rule should be applied during recitation.

What Is Tanween?

Tanween is the double vowel ending written on Arabic words. It produces a Noon sound when the word is connected to the next word during recitation. The three forms of Tanween are Fathatan, Dammatan and Kasratan.

Although Tanween does not appear as the letter Noon, its pronunciation can follow the same four rules when another word follows it. This is why Noon Sakinah and Tanween are usually studied together in Tajweed.

The Four Rules of Noon Sakinah and Tanween

The four main rules are:

  • Izhar — clear pronunciation
  • Idgham — merging the Noon sound into the following letter
  • Iqlab — changing the Noon sound into a concealed Meem sound
  • Ikhfa — partially concealing the Noon sound

1. Izhar — Clear Pronunciation

Izhar means to make the Noon Sakinah or Tanween sound clear. Izhar occurs when Noon Sakinah or Tanween is followed by one of the six throat letters:

ء ه ع ح غ خ

When one of these letters follows Noon Sakinah or Tanween, the Noon sound is pronounced clearly without merging it into the following letter.

For example, when Noon Sakinah or Tanween is followed by a throat letter, the reader should maintain a clear Noon sound before moving to the next letter.

For a detailed explanation and additional examples, see our guide to Izhar in Tajweed .

2. Idgham — Merging

Idgham means merging one sound into another. Noon Sakinah or Tanween can be merged when followed by one of the Idgham letters:

ي ر م ل و ن

These letters are commonly remembered through the Arabic grouping يرملون.

Idgham has two main types: Idgham with Ghunnah and Idgham without Ghunnah. The pronunciation depends on which Idgham letter follows Noon Sakinah or Tanween.

Learn the detailed rules and examples in our Idgham guide .

3. Iqlab — Changing Noon into Meem

Iqlab occurs when Noon Sakinah or Tanween is followed by the letter Ba (ب). In this situation, the Noon sound changes into a concealed Meem sound with Ghunnah.

The key letter for Iqlab is therefore:

ب

When reading an example of Iqlab, the student should avoid pronouncing a clear Noon. Instead, the sound is changed and concealed before the Ba.

You can learn more about this rule in our detailed Iqlab in Tajweed guide .

4. Ikhfa — Concealment

Ikhfa means partial concealment. It occurs when Noon Sakinah or Tanween is followed by one of the fifteen Ikhfa letters.

The Noon sound is neither pronounced completely clearly nor fully merged into the following letter. Instead, it is partially concealed while maintaining Ghunnah.

Ikhfa is one of the most commonly encountered Noon Sakinah and Tanween rules, so regular listening and guided practice can be helpful for students learning to apply it naturally.

For the complete list of letters and Quran examples, see our Ikhfa guide .

Noon Sakinah vs. Tanween

Noon Sakinah and Tanween are different in written form, but they share the same four main Tajweed rules when they are followed by different Arabic letters.

  • Noon Sakinah: the actual letter Noon with a sukoon.
  • Tanween: a double vowel ending that produces a Noon sound when connected to the following word.

Learning the two together makes it easier to identify the correct Tajweed rule while reading continuously.

How to Identify the Correct Rule

A simple way to identify the correct rule is to look at what comes immediately after Noon Sakinah or Tanween.

  1. Identify Noon Sakinah or Tanween.
  2. Look at the next Arabic letter.
  3. Check which of the four rule groups contains that letter.
  4. Apply the appropriate pronunciation while maintaining the correct Ghunnah when required.

With practice, students can begin recognizing these patterns automatically while reading the Quran.

Why Are These Rules Important?

Tajweed rules help Quran students pronounce Arabic letters and sounds correctly during recitation. Noon Sakinah and Tanween appear frequently in Quranic text, so understanding their rules can make reading more accurate and consistent.

Students do not need to memorize every rule at once. It is often easier to study one rule at a time, listen to examples and then practice applying the rule while reading short Quranic passages.
