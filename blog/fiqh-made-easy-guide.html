What is Fiqh in Islam?

Fiqh linguistically means "deep understanding". In Sharia, it means understanding practical Islamic rulings from detailed evidences - like how to pray, fast, pay Zakat, halal food, marriage. It answers: Is this wajib, sunnah, mubah, makruh, or haram?

Why Fiqh is Important?

Prophet ﷺ said: "When Allah wishes good for someone, He gives him understanding (fiqh) of the religion" [Bukhari & Muslim]. Without Fiqh, your Salah might be invalid, your Zakat wrong, your fasting broken. Fiqh protects your worship from mistakes.

2 Main Types of Fiqh

1. Fiqh al-Ibadat (Worship)

How to worship: Taharah (purity), Salah (prayer), Zakat (charity), Sawm (fasting), Hajj. This is 70% of what beginners need. Example: Learn 5 Pillars and correct wudu steps, times of prayer, what breaks wudu.

2. Fiqh al-Muamalat (Dealings)

Halal and haram in daily life: halal earning, riba, halal food in USA/UK supermarkets, marriage, divorce, inheritance. Very important for Muslims living in West where many foods and transactions contain haram.

4 Sources of Fiqh

  1. Quran - Primary source. Example: "And establish prayer" [Quran 2:43]
  2. Sunnah - How Prophet ﷺ prayed, fasted. Explains Quran.
  3. Ijma - Consensus of scholars.
  4. Qiyas - Analogical reasoning for new issues (like vaping, cryptocurrency) based on original rulings.

Fiqh in Daily Life for Muslims in USA & UK

  • Wudu at work/school: How to do masah over socks? When tayammum allowed?
  • Salah times: Combining prayers when traveling or at university?
  • Halal food: Is chicken at Walmart halal? What about gelatin, enzymes?
  • Zakat in USA: Do you pay Zakat on 401k, stocks? See 8 categories of Zakat.
  • Fasting: Long summer days in UK - how to fast?

All these are answered in Fiqh.
