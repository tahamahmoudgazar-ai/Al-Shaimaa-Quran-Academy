Prayer Times in Islam: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib & Isha

Prayer times in Islam are determined by the position of the sun. Muslims perform five obligatory prayers every day, and each prayer has its own appointed time.

🕐 Remember: Exact prayer clock times change by location and date, so use a reliable local timetable for your area.

Fajr Prayer Time

Fajr begins at true dawn and continues until sunrise. Muslims should complete the Fajr prayer before sunrise.

Dhuhr Prayer Time

Dhuhr begins after the sun passes its highest point in the sky. Its exact clock time changes throughout the year.

Asr Prayer Time

Asr takes place during the afternoon. There are differences in some details concerning the calculation of Asr time between Islamic schools of jurisprudence.

Maghrib Prayer Time

Maghrib begins immediately after sunset. Its beginning is closely connected to the disappearance of the sun below the horizon.

Isha Prayer Time

Isha begins after twilight disappears. Its exact time depends on the location and season.

Why Do Prayer Times Change?

Because Islamic prayer times are linked to the sun, the clock time of each prayer changes according to geographical location and season. Prayer times can therefore be very different between countries and even between cities.

How Can You Find Accurate Prayer Times?

  • Use a reliable local mosque timetable.
  • Use a trusted prayer-time application.
  • Check the calculation method used by the timetable.
  • Follow the guidance of your local Islamic community when needed.
